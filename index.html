Classical Foundations

Shielding & Effective Nuclear Charge

Z_eff = Z − σ
Z = atomic number · σ = shielding constant

Shielding ability: s > p > d > f > g

  • f and g orbitals are diffuse with many radial nodes → they penetrate poorly and shield weakly.
  • Lanthanoid contraction (Z=57–71): poor 4f shielding pulls in 6s → Zr and Hf end up nearly identical in size.
  • Actinoid contraction (Z=89–103): same effect from 5f, stabilising 7s in Ra and Lr.
  • In Element 120: 120 protons act almost unshielded beyond filled 5f¹⁴ 6d¹⁰ 7p⁶ — Z_eff on the 1s electrons approaches the full Z ≈ 120.

Step 3 · Relativistic Quantum Dynamics

Mechanics of 8s Stabilization

From electron velocity to orbital contraction — four linked steps.

1

Near-light inner velocity

v₁ₛ ≈ (Z/137.036)c ≈ 0.8757c

1s electrons at Z=120 travel ~87.6% of light speed.

2

Relativistic mass increase

m_rel = m₀ / √(1 − v²/c²)

Lorentz factor γ ≈ 2.08 — electron mass roughly doubles.

3

Bohr radius shrinkage

a₀ = ℏ² / (mₑe²)

Radius is inversely proportional to mass — the 1s cloud contracts sharply.

4

Orthogonality drags 8s inward

8s ⊥ 1s, 2s … 7s

Pauli exclusion forces every higher s-orbital to stay orthogonal to inner ones — so 8s is pulled in and its energy drops, binding the 8s² pair tightly.

Access to +4 / +6 Oxidation States

  • Normal Group 2 metals form only +2 — their core shells are too tightly bound to bond.
  • In Element 120, direct 8s contraction pushes indirect (shielding) effects onto 7p₃/₂ and 6d — these subshells expand and rise in energy.
  • That lets core electrons participate in bonding — giving stable +4 and +6 states in oxidizing conditions, unheard of for true alkaline earth metals.
Step 5 · Bring This Up Proactively

The Nuclear Volume Effect

Mentioning this before your teacher asks — and showing how R = r₀A^(1/3) softens central-field attraction — demonstrates complete mastery of the material.

R = r₀ A^(1/3)
r₀ ≈ 1.2 fm, A = Z + N (mass number)
  • More neutrons → larger A → the nucleus expands physically.
  • 120 protons now spread over a bigger volume, not a point charge.
  • s-electrons have non-zero probability AT the nucleus (ψ(0) ≠ 0), so they feel weaker attraction inside a spread-out charge.
  • This softens binding energy — directly opposing relativistic contraction.
  • ²⁹⁸Ubn vs ³⁰⁴Ubn: calculations show measurable eV-fraction shifts in ionization energy between isotopes.

How Far Can the Table Go?

Disproving the "Z = 137" Myth

The Z = 137 Myth
E₁ = mₑc²√(1 − (Zα)²)
  • Assumes a point-charge nucleus of zero radius.
  • At Z=137, Zα ≈ 1 — energy becomes imaginary for Z > 137.
  • This is a mathematical artifact, not physical law.
The Real Boundary: Z ≈ 172–173
Finite nuclear radius removes the singularity
  • Dirac-Fock models (Pyykkö, Fricke) extend solutions to Z ≈ 172.
  • At Z ≈ 173, 1s binding energy reaches −1.022 MeV — the Dirac-sea threshold — triggering spontaneous e⁻e⁺ pair creation.
Practical Lab Limit: Z ≈ 120–126
IUPAC: nucleus must survive > 10⁻¹⁴ s
  • Coulomb repulsion (∝Z²) beats nuclear binding (∝A) past Z≈120.
  • Spontaneous fission wins — capping synthesis near Z≈120–126, close to the predicted Island of Stability (N=184).
Not a New Idea

Relativity Already Breaks Period 7

Z=118

Oganesson

Group 18

Massive spin-orbit coupling smears the electron cloud into a near-uniform gas — Og loses its noble-gas energy gap and gains real reactivity.
